    Quiz: Story Creation & Templates¶

    Test your understanding of the story creation process, testing, maintenance, templates, and modular components with these 10 review questions.

    1. What is the correct sequence of stages in the story creation process?¶

    1. Ideation → Drafting → Refinement → Testing → Approval → Deployment → Maintenance → Retirement
    2. Drafting → Ideation → Testing → Refinement → Approval → Deployment → Retirement → Maintenance
    3. Deployment → Ideation → Drafting → Refinement → Testing → Approval → Maintenance → Retirement
    4. Ideation → Testing → Drafting → Refinement → Approval → Deployment → Maintenance → Retirement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. The process moves from generating concepts, to writing, refining, validating, approving, releasing, maintaining, and eventually retiring outdated stories. Testing before refinement or deploying before drafting would undermine quality and consistency.

    Concept Tested: Story Creation Process

    2. What is the typical gate criterion between the Refinement stage and the Testing stage?¶

    1. Concept approval
    2. Customer feedback
    3. A refined draft ready for validation
    4. Stakeholder sign-off for release
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The transition from refinement to testing requires a refined draft that is complete enough to validate with prospects or internal stakeholders. Concept approval precedes drafting, and final sign-off follows testing.

    Concept Tested: Story Refinement

    3. Why is it important to test stories before full deployment?¶

    1. To eliminate the approval process
    2. To validate that the story works in practice and catches issues early
    3. To avoid any need for maintenance
    4. To guarantee immediate retirement
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Testing reveals practical problems such as a weak hook, unclear agitation, or unconvincing social proof that refinement may miss. It does not remove approval, eliminate maintenance, or lead to retirement.

    Concept Tested: Story Testing

    4. During story drafting, what should the first draft prioritize?¶

    1. Perfect grammar and polished visuals
    2. Legal review and brand approval
    3. Final scripting for verbatim delivery
    4. Getting the core narrative structure down
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. The first draft should focus on a complete narrative structure—hook, problem, agitation, solution, social proof, and call to action—rather than perfection. Grammar, visuals, legal review, and scripting come in later stages.

    Concept Tested: Story Drafting

    5. Which activity is part of story maintenance?¶

    1. Creating a story once and never revisiting it
    2. Updating statistics, examples, and case studies as conditions change
    3. Deleting every story after one quarter
    4. Removing all tags to simplify search
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Maintenance keeps stories current by refreshing data, adding new case studies, and retiring outdated content. Neglecting stories, mass deletion, or removing tags would degrade the library's usefulness.

    Concept Tested: Story Maintenance

    6. What is the main benefit of modular story components?¶

    1. They eliminate the need for any customization
    2. They allow pre-approved story elements to be mixed and matched efficiently
    3. They bypass approval and testing requirements
    4. They restrict teams to a single story per product
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Modular components let salespeople assemble stories from pre-written, approved hooks, problems, agitations, and other elements, balancing efficiency with quality. They do not remove customization, approval, or variety.

    Concept Tested: Modular Story Components

    7. How do story templates differ from story frameworks?¶

    1. Templates are reusable structures with placeholders; frameworks are flexible high-level guides
    2. Templates and frameworks are identical
    3. Frameworks are only used for visual storyboards
    4. Templates prevent any creative adaptation
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Templates provide concrete reusable structures with specific placeholders, while frameworks offer high-level direction such as setup-conflict-resolution or teach-tailor-take control without prescribing every detail.

    Concept Tested: Story Templates

    8. A story draft has a vague problem statement and generic social proof. Which testing criterion is most clearly failing?¶

    1. Relevance
    2. Effectiveness
    3. Authenticity
    4. Clarity
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Vague language and lack of specifics make the story unclear and unconvincing. While relevance, effectiveness, and authenticity may also suffer, clarity is the most directly violated criterion because the message is not concrete.

    Concept Tested: Story Testing

    9. How should salespeople use story scripts in live delivery?¶

    1. Internalize the script so they can deliver it naturally
    2. Read every word verbatim from the page
    3. Avoid scripts entirely because they reduce authenticity
    4. Use them only for legal disclaimers
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Scripts are training tools that help salespeople learn challenging stories and ensure consistency. They should be practiced until delivery feels natural, not read like a teleprompter or ignored as inauthentic.

    Concept Tested: Story Scripting

    10. A team designs elaborate visuals first and then writes a narrative to fit those visuals. Which story visualization guideline has been violated?¶

    1. Visuals should be complex to impress audiences
    2. Visualization should follow and support the narrative
    3. Every story needs at least one animation
    4. Visuals should replace the spoken story entirely
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Effective visualization starts with the narrative and then identifies which points benefit from visual support. Designing visuals first risks creating disconnected or confusing graphics that do not serve the story.

    Concept Tested: Story Visualization
