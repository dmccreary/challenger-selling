    About This Book¶

    Welcome from Story the Lion¶

    Welcome!

    Hi, I'm Story the Lion, and I'll be your guide through this book. Buyers forget most of the slides they see, but they remember a good story about a problem they didn't know they had. In these chapters you'll learn to teach, tailor, and take control, and then wrap each insight in a narrative your buyers will retell to their own colleagues. Let's craft a story!

    Why This Intelligent Textbook¶

    B2B buying has changed faster than most sales training has. Buyers do most of their research before they ever talk to a seller, and they decide in large committees where each member has done their own research. A seller who shows up with a feature list adds little. A seller who brings a new insight about the buyer's business, told as a story the committee can repeat, is the one who shapes the decision.

    What the research says about Challenger selling:

    • In the Corporate Executive Board (CEB) study of more than 6,000 sales representatives, nearly 40% of star performers fit the Challenger profile, and in complex-solution sales that share rose to 54%1
    • Relationship Builders, the profile many sales leaders invest in most, made up only 7% of star performers overall2
    • More than 53% of B2B customer loyalty is driven by the sales experience itself, not by brand, product, service, or price3

    What buyers face today:

    • When B2B buyers are considering a purchase, they spend only 17% of that time meeting with potential suppliers, and as little as 5–6% with any one sales rep4
    • The typical buying group for a complex B2B solution involves six to ten decision makers, and 77% of buyers describe their latest purchase as very complex or difficult4
    • Gartner predicts that by 2028, 60% of B2B seller work will be done through conversational interfaces powered by generative AI, up from less than 5% in 20235

    The workforce this affects:

    • The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics counts 1,613,600 wholesale and manufacturing sales representative jobs (2024), with about 142,100 openings projected each year through 20346

    Behind these numbers are sellers who get a few minutes with a buying committee and need those minutes to count. This textbook helps them make each one count.

    This book takes a different approach from a traditional sales manual. It is built on a learning graph of 400 interconnected concepts. Each concept is introduced only after the concepts it depends on, so the Challenger methodology, storytelling psychology, buyer personas, and AI-assisted story generation build on each other chapter by chapter. Case studies come from products that are hard to explain, such as graph databases, AI frameworks, and intelligent textbook tools, because those are the sales where storytelling matters most. Interactive MicroSims let you practice key skills in the browser, such as mapping stakeholders, choosing an insight type, and comparing sales profiles. The entire textbook is open source and free, with no paywalls and no access codes.

    How to Use This Book¶

    This textbook is designed for self-paced study by sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers, and business development managers. Each chapter builds on earlier material, so reading in order is recommended. The book includes:

    • 17 Chapters covering Challenger fundamentals, storytelling psychology, buyer personas, objection handling, AI-assisted story generation, product case studies, story effectiveness analytics, delivery and coaching, story libraries, and a capstone project
    • Interactive MicroSims that let you explore the five sales profiles, build customer priority maps, map stakeholders, and select insight types
    • Glossary with definitions for key concepts
    • Learning Graph showing the dependencies among 400 concepts
    • Search available from any page using the search bar

    The Learning Graph shows how concepts connect across chapters. If you want to explore out of order, or check the prerequisites for a specific topic, start there.

    About the Author¶

    Dan McCreary is a semi-retired AI researcher, solution architect, and educator who has spent more than three decades helping Fortune 100 organizations reason over massive datasets. At Optum he founded the Generative AI Center of Excellence and led the team that built one of the world's largest healthcare knowledge graphs — spanning over 25 billion vertices — to unify member, provider, and patient insights. Dan's deep background in knowledge representation and systems thinking underpins the precise learning graphs and intelligent textbook workflows used throughout this course.

    He is the co-author of Making Sense of NoSQL (Manning Publications), the founding chair of the NoSQL Now! conference, and a frequent keynote speaker on semantic search, ontology strategy, and AI hardware. Beyond industry, Dan has mentored students as a STEM volunteer since 2014 and now applies the same rigor to building open educational resources.     How to Cite This Book¶

    If you reference this textbook in academic work, sales training programs, enablement proposals, or other publications, please use one of the following citation formats.

    APA (7th edition)

    McCreary, D. (2026). Challenger Selling. https://dmccreary.github.io/challenger-selling/

    Chicago (17th edition)

    McCreary, Dan. 2026. Challenger Selling. https://dmccreary.github.io/challenger-selling/.

    MLA (9th edition)

    McCreary, Dan. Challenger Selling. 2026, dmccreary.github.io/challenger-selling/.

    BibTeX

    @book{mccreary2026challenger,
  title     = {Challenger Selling},
  author    = {McCreary, Dan},
  year      = {2026},
  url       = {https://dmccreary.github.io/challenger-selling/},
  note      = {Interactive intelligent textbook}
}

    To cite a specific chapter, append the chapter number and title — for example:

    McCreary, D. (2026). Chapter 1: Challenger Sales Methodology & Insights. In Challenger Selling. https://dmccreary.github.io/challenger-selling/chapters/01-challenger-methodology-insights/
