# Cover Image Prompt

Please generate a professional-quality cover image for this textbook.
This image will be used in social media previews and must follow the
formatting guidelines for an Open Graph image preview.

**Required specifications:**
- Format: PNG
- Wide-landscape format
- Size: 1200x630 pixels (1.91:1 aspect ratio)
- This is the Open Graph standard for social media previews

The image has four layers, back to front: background montage, color
treatment, mascot, and title text.

## Subject & Tone

Challenger Selling is a textbook about combining the Challenger Sales methodology with the power of storytelling to create memorable, persuasive presentations for sales professionals. The course teaches how to craft compelling narratives, use AI tools to generate sales scenarios, and build interactive agents that simulate real sales conversations. The intended audience is sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers, business development managers, and technical sales consultants. The visual tone should be modern, professional, confident, and strategic — reflecting both the analytical nature of sales methodology and the emotional power of storytelling.

## Title

Place Challenger Selling in the center of the image, in a clean, highly legible
sans-serif font. Use a light/white font color with a subtle drop shadow or
dark scrim behind it so it stays readable against the busy montage
background. Keep the title short enough to render at a large size — do not
shrink it to fit if the title is long; instead simplify the background
directly behind the text.

## Background Montage

Arrange a montage of the following 8 concepts around the title, each
rendered in a consistent illustration style (see Style below) so the
composition reads as one image rather than a collage of unrelated styles:

1. Challenger Sales Methodology — a three-part diagram showing teaching, tailoring, and taking control as interconnected gears or arrows in a circular flow
2. Hero's Journey — a simplified story arc with ascending and descending curves, marked with key stages like "challenge," "transformation," and "resolution"
3. Buyer Persona Mapping — a row of stylized icons representing different stakeholders (CIO, CTO, CFO, CEO) with connecting lines showing their relationships
4. AI-Assisted Story Generation — a stylized neural network or brain illustration with glowing nodes and edges, suggesting AI intelligence
5. Graph Database — a classic node-and-edge visualization with colored dots connected by lines, showing data relationships
6. Story Effectiveness Measurement — a clean analytics dashboard with simple bar charts and metrics icons
7. Interactive Sales Agents — a chat interface or dialogue bubble visualization showing conversation flow
8. Story Portfolio Management — a library or folder metaphor with stacked cards or documents representing organized stories

## Mascot

Place the book's mascot in the lower-left corner, sized so it does not
overlap the title text. The mascot is Story the Lion: a friendly brown lion (hex #8B4513) with gold accents (hex #FFD700), no clothing, a confident smile, and a thoughtful pose. The art style is flat cartoon with modern vector clean lines. The lion should look approachable and motivational, embodying the confidence and leadership of the Challenger Sales methodology.

## Style & Composition

- Illustration style: flat vector illustration with clean lines and modern aesthetic — choose one style and apply it to every montage element for visual consistency
- Color palette: deep blue and teal with amber/gold accents (mimicking the lion's gold), reflecting professional technology sales while being warm and approachable
- Lighting/mood: bright and optimistic, with a sense of forward momentum and confidence
- Composition: title centered, montage elements arranged in a loose ring or grid around it, mascot in the lower-left, generous negative space immediately behind the title so it stays readable

## Avoid

- Do not render dense paragraphs of illegible text anywhere in the image.
- Avoid generic stock-photo cliches (handshakes, isolated lightbulbs, people pointing at whiteboards) unless one is explicitly one of the chosen concepts.
- Avoid photorealistic human faces unless the book's subject calls for a specific recognizable figure.
- Do not let montage elements visually compete with or overlap the title.
