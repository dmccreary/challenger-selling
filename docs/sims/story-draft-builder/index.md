## About This MicroSim

A first draft is about **structure**, not polish. If the hook, problem, agitation, solution, social proof, and call to action are all there in the right order, the story can be refined. If one is missing, no amount of polish will save it.

In this MicroSim you write a draft about a manufacturer that cut downtime by 60%. Each component has a drafting tip and a target length. When you submit, your draft is assembled in order, and a checklist looks for structural signals: a number or question in the hook, a quantified problem, consequences in the agitation, concrete implementation in the solution, a number or quote in the proof, and one specific ask in the call to action. You can then compare your draft with an example.

**Learning objective (Apply, *construct*):** Construct a story draft by writing content for each story component (hook, problem, agitation, solution, social proof, call to action) following drafting best practices.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario and the tip for each component.
2. Write each of the six components (at least a sentence each).
3. Select **Assemble My Draft** to see the full draft and the structural checklist.
4. Select **Compare With an Example**, or **Revise My Draft** to improve it.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
15 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Drafting section of Chapter 12

### Activities

1. **Drafting** (8 min): Write all six components without stopping to polish.
2. **Revision** (4 min): Fix every checklist item marked &#9675; and reassemble.
3. **Peer review** (3 min): Read your draft aloud to a partner. Which component is weakest?

### Common Misconceptions
- Drafts should be perfect.
- Components can be in any order.
- Details are optional.

### Assessment
- All six components completed in order.
- At least five of six structural checks pass after revision.
