## About This MicroSim

Return on investment (ROI) is the language leadership uses to approve storytelling budgets: (total return &minus; total investment) &divide; total investment.

In this MicroSim you first calculate the ROI of a $50,000 story library: total the costs, total the benefits (including efficiency savings, not just revenue), and compute the percentage. Then a what-if panel challenges the headline number. How much of the $200,000 was really caused by stories? What if you count gross margin instead of revenue? And does the result clear your organization's hurdle rate? Watching 360% shrink as the assumptions get stricter is the point: ROI is built on estimates.

**Learning objective (Apply, *calculate*):** Calculate ROI for a storytelling initiative by identifying investment costs, return benefits, and computing the ROI ratio.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario and the cost and benefit lines.
2. Enter the total investment, total return, and ROI percentage.
3. Select **Check My Calculation**. After two tries a worked answer appears.
4. In Step 4, move the attribution slider, toggle margin, and change the hurdle rate to see how the conclusion changes.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
12 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story ROI Models and Story Cost-Benefit Analysis sections of Chapter 14

### Activities

1. **Calculation** (4 min): Calculate the ROI correctly.
2. **Sensitivity** (5 min): Find the attribution level at which the ROI drops below a 100% hurdle rate, with and without margin.
3. **Communication** (3 min): Write one sentence presenting the ROI to a CFO that states your key assumption.

### Common Misconceptions
- ROI is only about revenue.
- ROI calculation is exact.
- Any positive ROI is good.

### Assessment
- Correct totals and a 360% ROI.
- Learner can explain why a positive ROI may still fail a hurdle rate.
