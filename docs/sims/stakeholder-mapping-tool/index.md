## About This MicroSim

Complex sales involve several stakeholders, and each plays two roles at once. Their **buyer type** describes what they decide on: budget (economic), technical fit (technical), or daily use (user). Their **influence type** describes how they behave in the process: guiding you (coach), driving change (mobilizer), demanding evidence (skeptic), or supporting quietly (friend).

In this MicroSim you are selling an intelligent textbook platform to a university. For each of four stakeholders you choose both types. Feedback explains the classification and corrects common mix-ups, such as calling a supportive stakeholder a mobilizer, or treating skeptics as opponents. The summary shows whom to prioritize and how to engage each one.

**Learning objective (Apply, *categorize*):** Categorize stakeholders into buyer types (Economic, Technical, User) and influence types (Coach, Mobilizer, Skeptic, Friend) for a given sales scenario.

## How to Use

1. Read the stakeholder's description.
2. Choose a **Buyer Type** and an **Influence Type**.
3. Select **Submit** for feedback. You can resubmit; only your first try counts.
4. Select **Next Stakeholder**. After all four, the summary shows how to engage each stakeholder.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Stakeholder definitions (Economic, Technical, and User Buyer; Coach, Mobilizer, Skeptic, Friend) in Chapter 1

### Activities

1. **Mapping** (5 min): Categorize all four stakeholders and record your first-try score.
2. **Distinction** (2 min): Explain the difference between a Mobilizer and a Friend in one sentence.
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Map the stakeholders in one of your current deals using both types.

### Common Misconceptions
- Economic buyers are always the most important.
- Mobilizers and friends are the same.
- Skeptics are opponents.

### Assessment
- Correct buyer and influence type for all four stakeholders.
- Learner can explain why skeptics should be engaged with data, not avoided.
