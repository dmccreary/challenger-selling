## About This MicroSim

A sales story has six components: **Opening Hook**, **Problem Statement**, **Agitation**, **Solution Presentation**, **Social Proof**, and **Call to Action**. Their order matters. Tension builds through the problem and its consequences, and only then does the solution release it. Skip the agitation or move the solution earlier, and the customer never feels why the solution matters.

This MicroSim has two steps. In **Step 1** you drag six shuffled components into the story sequence. Misplaced slots turn red, and a targeted hint explains the most important ordering error. In **Step 2** you write one or two sentences for each component about a predictive maintenance platform for a manufacturer. When you submit, the assembled story is shown along with feedback, which includes a check for a vague call to action and for missing numbers.

**Learning objective (Apply, *construct*):** Construct a sales story by arranging the six story components in the correct sequence and writing content for each component.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario.
2. Drag each component into a numbered slot. You can also click a component and then click a slot. Dropping on a filled slot swaps the two components; drop a component back on the pool to remove it.
3. When all six slots are filled, select **Check Sequence**. Fix any red slots and check again.
4. Select **Continue: Write Your Story** and write 1-2 sentences per component. **Show example** puts a model sentence in the box as placeholder text.
5. Select **Submit Story** to see your assembled story and feedback.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
15 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Sales Story Components section of Chapter 4

### Activities

1. **Sequencing** (3 min): Order the six components. Note the hint you received, if any.
2. **Writing** (8 min): Write all six components for the predictive maintenance scenario.
3. **Peer review** (4 min): Swap stories with a partner. Is the call to action specific enough to put on a calendar?

### Common Misconceptions
- Components can be in any order.
- Some components are optional.
- The call to action can be vague.

### Assessment
- All six components in the correct sequence.
- Each written component fulfills its role; the call to action names a concrete next step.
