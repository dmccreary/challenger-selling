## About This MicroSim

Good storytelling training is **progressive**: theory first, then practice, then feedback, then advanced work. It mixes practice formats, because reps need both individual repetitions and the chance to learn from peers. And it does not end when the workshop does.

In this MicroSim you design a program to take a sales team from novice to intermediate storytelling in eight weeks. Select four to six components, put them in order with the arrow buttons, and choose a practice format for each hands-on component. Feedback flags missing theory or practice, out-of-order sequences, and all-individual or all-group practice. A valid design produces a week-by-week schedule that ends with an ongoing monthly story clinic.

**Learning objective (Apply, *design*):** Design a storytelling training program by selecting training components, sequencing them appropriately, and defining practice activities.

## How to Use

1. Read the training objective.
2. Step 1: check 4&ndash;6 training components. Each is tagged theory, practice, feedback, or advanced.
3. Step 2: use the &uarr; and &darr; buttons to order the components.
4. Step 3: choose a practice format for each practice and feedback component.
5. Select **Build Schedule**. Fix any problems listed, or review the weekly schedule. Use **Edit Program** to revise.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
12 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Sales Team Training and Story Workshops sections of Chapter 10

### Activities

1. **Design** (5 min): Build a valid program and review the schedule.
2. **Experiment** (3 min): Move the advanced workshop to the top and rebuild. Read the feedback.
3. **Transfer** (4 min): Adapt the schedule for a team that already has the fundamentals. Which components would you drop?

### Common Misconceptions
- Training is one-time.
- Theory is sufficient.
- All training is the same.

### Assessment
- A valid program: theory and practice present, a progressive sequence, and a mix of individual and group practice.
- Learner can explain why coaching works best as expert feedback.
