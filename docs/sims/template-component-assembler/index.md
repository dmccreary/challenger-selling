## About This MicroSim

Modular story components let teams assemble stories quickly, but only when the components share one theme and follow the story structure. A competitive-threat hook does not belong in a cost story for a CFO, and a call to action does not belong first.

In this MicroSim the component library is shown in scrambled order, so it does not hint at the structure. You pick one hook, problem, agitation, solution, social proof, and call to action, then arrange your picks with the arrow buttons. Checking the assembly flags both order problems and components that do not fit the CFO cost objective, and shows the assembled story with any misfits highlighted.

**Learning objective (Apply, *assemble*):** Assemble a complete story by selecting and arranging modular story components (hook, problem, agitation, solution, social proof, call to action) from a component library.

## How to Use

1. Read the story objective.
2. Step 1: pick one component from each category in the library.
3. Step 2: use &uarr; and &darr; to arrange your picks into story order.
4. Select **Check Assembly**. Fix flagged items and check again.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Templates and Modular Story Components sections of Chapter 12

### Activities

1. **Assembly** (5 min): Build a coherent story and record how many checks it took.
2. **Swap test** (2 min): Swap in Social Proof B. Why does the story weaken for a CFO even though it is still positive?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Re-assemble the story for a CTO using the B components. What changes?

### Common Misconceptions
- Components can be in any order.
- Any components work together.
- Assembly replaces creativity.

### Assessment
- All six components fit the objective and are in story order.
- Learner can explain why a demo is a weaker call to action for a CFO than an ROI analysis.
