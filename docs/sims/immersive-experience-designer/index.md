## About This MicroSim

Virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and mixed reality (MR) solve different problems. VR replaces the surroundings, so it can bring a remote prospect into a factory. AR overlays information on the real world, so it only helps someone standing in front of the equipment. MR anchors interactive virtual objects in a real room.

In this MicroSim you design an immersive experience to show a new production line to prospects who will never visit the plant. You choose the technology, the environment, the interaction, and the story focus. The specification explains each choice and runs a **consistency check**, flagging combinations that cannot work together, such as an overlay on the real factory paired with VR.

**Learning objective (Apply, *design*):** Design an immersive storytelling experience by selecting the appropriate technology (VR, AR, MR) and defining the experience elements for a given scenario.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario, noting who the audience is and where they are.
2. Choose a technology and the three experience elements.
3. Select **Generate Specification** to see feedback, the specification, and the consistency check.
4. Change any choice and regenerate to compare designs.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Virtual Reality, Story Augmented Reality, and Story Mixed Reality sections of Chapter 16

### Activities

1. **Design** (4 min): Design the experience and read the specification.
2. **Variation** (3 min): Change the scenario mentally: prospects are now visiting the plant. Which choices change?
3. **Discussion** (3 min): Which of your customers could you show your solution to in VR?

### Common Misconceptions
- All immersive tech is the same.
- VR is always best.
- Immersive is just visual.

### Assessment
- Recommended technology and elements chosen, with no consistency conflicts.
- Learner can explain when AR is a better choice than VR.
