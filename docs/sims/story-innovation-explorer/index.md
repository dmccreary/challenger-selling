## About This MicroSim

Most sales stories sound the same: a linear success story, told from the vendor's point of view, on a slide. **Story innovation** means making deliberate, strategic choices that make a story memorable.

In this MicroSim you redesign a standard story on four dimensions: structure (for example, *in medias res*), angle (for example, a market-wide shift), format (for example, an interactive walkthrough), and type of example (for example, a counterintuitive result). This is a Create-level task, so any differentiated choice is accepted; only the default, standard options are flagged. Your choices are assembled into a four-part story concept, with a prompt to test whether it is more memorable or just different.

**Learning objective (Create, *innovate*):** Innovate a new storytelling approach by selecting unconventional story elements (structure, angle, format, example) that differentiate from standard approaches.

## How to Use

1. Read the standard story.
2. Choose one option on each of the four dimensions.
3. Select **Build My Story Concept** to see feedback on each choice and the assembled concept.
4. Try different combinations and rebuild to compare concepts.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
12 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Innovation and Story Differentiation sections of Chapter 13

### Activities

1. **Design** (5 min): Build a concept that is differentiated on all four dimensions.
2. **Critique** (3 min): Build two different concepts. Which is more memorable, and which is just different?
3. **Pitch** (4 min): Tell your concept aloud in 60 seconds to a partner.

### Common Misconceptions
- Innovation means random changes.
- Standard approaches are bad.
- Any difference is innovation.

### Assessment
- A concept that is differentiated on all four dimensions.
- Learner can explain why innovation must serve the insight, not just change the format.
