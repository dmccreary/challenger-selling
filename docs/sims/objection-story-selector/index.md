## About This MicroSim

When a customer objects, the instinct is to argue. A story usually works better: it shows that someone else had the same concern and how it turned out, and it lets the customer reach the conclusion themselves. But the story has to fit the objection. A cost-of-delay story does nothing for a buyer who is worried about disruption.

This MicroSim presents five common objections. For each, you select one of the five story types from the chapter. Feedback explains what that story type does. A wrong choice gets a reason, including the tricky cases: an objection that mentions price can really be a competitor question. Every answer is followed by a short example story you could adapt.

**Learning objective (Apply, *select*):** Select the appropriate objection story type (Price, Timing, Competitor, Risk, Authority) for different customer objections.

## How to Use

1. Read the customer objection.
2. Choose a **Story Type** from the dropdown.
3. Select **Submit** to see feedback and an example story. You can resubmit to compare; only your first try is scored.
4. Select **Next Objection** to continue. The summary lists every objection with its story type.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Handling Objections with Stories section of Chapter 4

### Activities

1. **Selection** (4 min): Work through all five objections and record your first-try score.
2. **Analysis** (3 min): Objection 3 mentions a lower price. Why is it a Competitor objection and not a Price objection?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Write down the most common objection you hear, name its story type, and outline a story from your own customers.

### Common Misconceptions
- Any story can address any objection.
- Arguments work better than stories for objections.
- Objections should be countered directly.

### Assessment
- Correct story type for all five objections.
- Learner can distinguish a Price objection from a Competitor objection that mentions price.
