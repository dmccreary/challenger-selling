## About This MicroSim

Before a story goes into the library, it should be **tested** against clear criteria. This MicroSim gives you a deliberately weak draft ("A company faced challenges... achieved results. You should try it too.") and four criteria: clarity, relevance, effectiveness, and authenticity.

For each criterion you choose a rating and write feedback the author could act on. The summary puts your ratings and feedback next to an expert's, adds a specific improvement suggestion for each criterion, and shows an improved draft. The aim is to practice feedback that guides rather than criticizes.

**Learning objective (Evaluate, *evaluate*):** Evaluate a story against testing criteria (clarity, relevance, effectiveness, authenticity) and provide feedback for improvement.

## How to Use

1. Read the story draft.
2. For each criterion, choose a rating and write specific feedback for the author.
3. Select **See Test Summary** to compare your evaluation with the expert review.
4. Change any rating and resubmit to compare; your first-attempt score is kept.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
12 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Testing section of Chapter 12

### Activities

1. **Evaluation** (6 min): Rate and give feedback on all four criteria.
2. **Calibration** (3 min): Compare your feedback with the improvement suggestions. Was yours as specific?
3. **Application** (3 min): Test one story from your own library on the same four criteria.

### Common Misconceptions
- Testing is optional.
- One criterion is sufficient.
- Feedback is criticism.

### Assessment
- Ratings match the expert on all four criteria.
- Each feedback note names a specific change, not just a judgment.
