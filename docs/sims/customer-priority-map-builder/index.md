## About This MicroSim

A **Customer Priority Map** shows how different stakeholders rank the same competing concerns. In a complex sale, these priorities rarely agree: finance wants cost control, IT wants security and stability, and operations wants reliability and speed.

In this MicroSim you rank five priorities for three stakeholders at a manufacturing company. You can use the arrow buttons or drag rows. After each ranking you see how it compares with an expert map and why. Your three rankings then form a completed map, with rows flagged wherever stakeholders strongly disagree. Finally you test the teaching insight "brittle data infrastructure blocks innovation" against the map: where does it align, and where does it create tension? The analysis explains how to tailor the insight for each stakeholder.

**Learning objective (Apply, *construct*):** Construct a Customer Priority Map by ranking competing priorities for different stakeholders and identifying where a teaching insight aligns or creates tension.

## How to Use

1. Read the stakeholder's focus.
2. Rank the five priorities with the &uarr;/&darr; buttons, or drag a row onto another row.
3. Select **Submit Ranking** to compare with the expert map. Adjust and resubmit, or continue.
4. After all three stakeholders, review the completed map, then choose Aligns, Partially aligns, or Creates tension for each stakeholder.
5. Select **See Alignment Analysis** for the expert view and tailored messaging.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
15 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Customer Priority Map section of Chapter 1
- Stakeholder types defined in Chapter 1

### Activities

1. **Mapping** (6 min): Rank priorities for all three stakeholders.
2. **Conflict analysis** (4 min): Which priorities are flagged as conflicts? What would happen if you pitched the same message to all three stakeholders?
3. **Tailoring** (5 min): Write one sentence tailoring the insight for the stakeholder where it creates the most tension.

### Common Misconceptions
- All stakeholders prioritize the same things.
- Priorities are static.
- Insight alignment is always perfect.

### Assessment
- Top two priorities match the expert map for all three stakeholders.
- Correct alignment judgment for all three stakeholders.
