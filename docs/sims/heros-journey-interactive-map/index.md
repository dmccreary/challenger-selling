## About This MicroSim

The Hero's Journey is the story structure Joseph Campbell found in myths across cultures: a hero leaves the ordinary world, faces trials, and returns transformed. In a sales story the **customer is the hero** and the seller plays the **mentor**.

This MicroSim lays the 11 stages around a circle. The dashed line separates the customer's *known world* (the status quo) from the *special world* of change they enter once they cross the threshold, usually by agreeing to a pilot. Clicking a stage reveals what it means in the monomyth and how it maps onto a customer's journey from problem to case study.

**Learning objective (Understand, *explain*):** Explain the stages of the Hero's Journey and how they apply to sales storytelling.

## How to Use

1. Click any numbered stage (or its label) to reveal its description and sales application. Stages can be explored in any order.
2. Explored stages turn purple; the currently selected stage is orange. The progress bar tracks how many of the 11 you have opened.
3. After all 11 stages are explored, select **Start the Quiz**. Three questions appear one at a time in random order.
4. Each question allows one attempt. You see the correct answer and an explanation, then your score at the end. Choose **Retry Quiz** or **Review the Map**.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Hero's Journey section of Chapter 2
- Basic familiarity with a B2B sales cycle

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (5 min): Open all 11 stages. For each, jot down one moment from a recent deal that matches it.
2. **Guided Practice** (5 min): In pairs, retell a customer success story using only stages 1, 2, 4, 5, 9 and 11. Which stages could you drop without losing tension?
3. **Assessment** (5 min): Complete the quiz. Mastery is 3 of 3 correct on the first attempt.

### Common Misconceptions
- The Hero's Journey is only for epic myths.
- All stages must appear in every story.
- The salesperson is the hero.

### Assessment
- Learner identifies the Call to Adventure as the customer recognizing a problem.
- Learner identifies the customer, not the seller, as the hero.
- Learner maps a pilot agreement to Crossing the Threshold.
