## About This MicroSim

Buyers withhold trust for different reasons, and each reason needs a different kind of story. Doubts about your expertise call for a **credibility** story. A request for outside validation calls for an **authority** story. A rep who seems too polished needs a **vulnerability** story. Doubts about honesty call for an **authenticity** story.

This MicroSim gives you four trust concerns and asks you to choose the story type for each. Feedback explains what each type does, using the definitions from Chapter 11. The summary reminds you that authority is not always the strongest choice, and that trust builds over the whole sales cycle.

**Learning objective (Apply, *select*):** Select the appropriate trust story type (Credibility, Authority, Vulnerability, Authenticity) for different customer trust concerns.

## How to Use

1. Read the customer's trust concern.
2. Choose one of the four **Trust Story Type** buttons.
3. Select **Submit** for feedback. You can resubmit; only your first try counts.
4. Select **Next Concern**. After all four, the summary lists each concern and its story type.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
8 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Trust Building, Credibility Stories, and Authority Stories sections of Chapter 11

### Activities

1. **Matching** (4 min): Complete all four concerns and record your first-try score.
2. **Distinction** (2 min): Explain the difference between a credibility story and an authority story in one sentence.
3. **Transfer** (2 min): Write a two-sentence vulnerability story from your own selling experience.

### Common Misconceptions
- All trust stories are the same.
- Authority is always best.
- Trust is built instantly.

### Assessment
- Correct story type for all four concerns.
- Learner can explain why a vulnerability story can build more trust than an authority story.
