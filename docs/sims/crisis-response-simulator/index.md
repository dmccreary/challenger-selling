## About This MicroSim

When a crisis hits, the story you tell in the first few hours shapes everything that follows. Defensive moves (denying, deflecting, minimizing) feel safe in the moment and destroy trust later.

In this MicroSim a data breach has exposed 10,000 customer records and a reporter is waiting. You make four decisions: how to acknowledge, what to share, who is responsible, and what to emphasize. Your choices are assembled into the actual statement a spokesperson would read, tagged with the four principles: acknowledgment, transparency, accountability, and solution focus. Reading a defensive statement aloud is often more persuasive than any explanation.

**Learning objective (Apply, *respond*):** Respond to a crisis scenario by selecting appropriate crisis storytelling elements (acknowledgment, transparency, accountability, solution-focus) and avoiding defensive behaviors.

## How to Use

1. Read the crisis scenario.
2. Make a choice for each of the four decisions.
3. Select **Assemble the Statement** to see feedback and the press statement your choices produce.
4. Change any decision and reassemble to hear the difference.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Crisis Response and Story Reputation Management sections of Chapter 13

### Activities

1. **Decisions** (4 min): Make all four decisions and read the statement aloud.
2. **Contrast** (3 min): Build the most defensive statement possible and read it aloud. How would a customer react?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Draft a two-sentence acknowledgment for a service outage at your own company.

### Common Misconceptions
- Minimizing the crisis helps.
- Blame others is strategic.
- Speed is less important than accuracy.

### Assessment
- All four decisions follow the recommended principles.
- Learner can explain why "blame hackers" damages trust even when it is true.
