## About This MicroSim

Personalization is not adding the stakeholder's name to a slide. The underlying fact stays the same, but what you **emphasize** and what **context** you use to support it change with each stakeholder's priorities.

This MicroSim starts with one insight: legacy data systems consume 40% of IT budgets. For each of three stakeholders you choose an emphasis (cost savings, competitive advantage, operational efficiency, or strategic positioning) and a context (industry benchmark, financial metrics, case study, or risk scenario). After each submission the insight is rewritten from your choices, so you can see whether it would land with that person.

**Learning objective (Apply, *tailor*):** Tailor a Challenger insight for different stakeholders by selecting which aspects to emphasize and which context to provide.

## How to Use

1. Read the base insight and the stakeholder description.
2. Choose an **Emphasis** and a **Context** from the dropdowns.
3. Select **Submit**. Your personalized insight appears with feedback. Try other combinations and resubmit to compare; only the first try counts.
4. Select **Next Stakeholder**. After all three, the summary shows each stakeholder's best combination.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Insight Personalization section of Chapter 10

### Activities

1. **Personalizing** (5 min): Complete all three stakeholders and read each rewritten insight aloud.
2. **Contrast** (2 min): Submit the CFO's combination for the startup CTO. Why would it fall flat?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Choose an emphasis and context for a real stakeholder in one of your current deals.

### Common Misconceptions
- Personalization is just adding a name.
- The same insight works for everyone.
- Personalization is optional.

### Assessment
- Correct emphasis and context for all three stakeholders.
- Learner can explain why the healthcare COO needs a risk scenario.
