## About This MicroSim

Sentiment analysis reads the emotional tone of language. It is often easy ("thrilled," "disappointed") and sometimes subtle: "performs as expected" is neutral, not positive, and "cautiously optimistic... but we need to see" is positive with hedges.

In this MicroSim you label four customer excerpts as positive, negative, or neutral and explain which words signal the sentiment. After each submission the indicator words are highlighted by tone (positive, negative, neutral, hedge), and you see how many of them your explanation named. For sales, the hedges matter most: they show exactly what proof a customer still needs.

**Learning objective (Evaluate, *analyze*):** Analyze the sentiment of story excerpts and customer responses by identifying emotional tone (positive, negative, neutral) and explaining the sentiment indicators.

## How to Use

1. Read the customer excerpt.
2. Choose a **Sentiment** and write which words or phrases signal it.
3. Select **Submit** to see the highlighted indicators and feedback. You can resubmit; only your first try counts.
4. Select **Next Excerpt**. After all four, the summary lists each excerpt and its indicators.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Sentiment AI and Story Emotion AI sections of Chapter 16

### Activities

1. **Analysis** (5 min): Label all four excerpts and explain your indicators.
2. **Hedges** (2 min): Rewrite excerpt 4 to remove the hedges. How does the sentiment change?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Label the sentiment of the last email you received from a prospect, with indicators.

### Common Misconceptions
- Sentiment is obvious.
- Words alone determine sentiment.
- Sentiment is static.

### Assessment
- Correct sentiment for all four excerpts.
- Explanations name at least half of the indicators in each excerpt.
