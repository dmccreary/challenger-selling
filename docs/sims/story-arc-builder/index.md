## About This MicroSim

A story arc is the shape of a narrative's tension over time. Most sales stories follow four beats: a **setup** that establishes context and stakes, **rising action** where obstacles build tension, a **climax** at the peak decision point, and a **resolution** that delivers the outcome and its proof.

In this MicroSim a manufacturing company's adoption of an IoT platform has been broken into four shuffled sentences. Your job is to put them in arc order. When the arc is correct, a tension curve shows how engagement climbs to the climax and then releases into the resolution. A common mistake is to treat the decision as the starting point; here the CTO's approval is the climax because it is where the tension peaks.

**Learning objective (Apply, *construct*):** Construct a story arc by sequencing story elements (setup, rising action, climax, resolution) for a given sales scenario.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario and the four story element cards.
2. Drag each card into one of the four arc slots. You can also click a card and then click a slot, which works on touch screens and with the keyboard (Tab to a card, then Enter).
3. Dropping a card on an occupied slot sends the previous card back to the pool. Drag a card back to the pool to remove it.
4. When all four slots are filled, select **Check My Arc**. Misplaced cards are outlined in red; rearrange and check again.
5. A correct arc reveals the tension curve. Select **Shuffle and Reset** to try again.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Story Arc section of Chapter 2

### Activities

1. **Construction** (4 min): Build the arc and check it. Note how many attempts you needed.
2. **Reflection** (3 min): Why is the CTO's approval the climax rather than the setup? What would the story lose if it began with the 60% downtime result?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Write four one-sentence beats for one of your own customer stories and sequence them the same way.

### Common Misconceptions
- All stories follow the same arc.
- The climax always comes at the end.
- Setup is unimportant.

### Assessment
- All four elements are placed in the correct slot.
- Learner can explain why the climax is the peak of tension, not the end of the story.
