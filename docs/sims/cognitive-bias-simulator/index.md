## About This MicroSim

Buyers do not decide purely on the merits. Decision fatigue, status quo bias and the anchoring effect each distort a decision in a different way, and each calls for a different kind of story.

This MicroSim presents three realistic customer moments. For each, you diagnose the bias from a list of seven and pick one of three story strategies. Feedback explains *why* the right strategy works for that bias, and tells you when you got the bias right but the strategy wrong (or the reverse). A summary at the end pairs each bias with its story strategy.

**Learning objective (Apply, *identify*):** Identify which cognitive bias influences a customer's decision and select the appropriate story strategy to address it.

## How to Use

1. Read the scenario.
2. Choose a bias from the first dropdown and a story strategy from the second.
3. Select **Submit** to see feedback. Green means correct and red means incorrect; orange feedback means you got one of the two right.
4. You may change your selections and resubmit to compare feedback, but only your first attempt counts toward mastery.
5. Select **Next Scenario**. After the third scenario, review the summary table and select **Start Over** to practice again.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10-15 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Cognitive Biases and Decision-Making section of Chapter 2

### Activities

1. **Diagnosis** (6 min): Work through all three scenarios.
2. **Contrast** (4 min): For scenario 2, explain why a success story about similar companies is weaker than a story about a competitor who failed. Which bias does each story engage?
3. **Transfer** (5 min): Describe a stalled deal you have seen. Which of the seven biases was at work, and what story would you tell next?

### Common Misconceptions
- All biases work the same way.
- Biases are always negative.
- Stories cannot overcome biases.

### Assessment
- Learner correctly identifies the bias and strategy for all three scenarios on the first attempt.
- Learner can explain how loss aversion helps overcome status quo bias.
