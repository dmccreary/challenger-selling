## About This MicroSim

A **learning graph** maps concepts as nodes and prerequisites as arrows. In this MicroSim an arrow points *from* the prerequisite *to* the concept that depends on it, so the graph tells you what to learn first.

The example has four concepts from this book: Storytelling Fundamentals, Challenger Methodology, Story Delivery, and Story Analytics. Click each arrow to reveal its relationship. Then answer five questions about direct prerequisites, what can be learned when, a valid overall order, and whether two concepts can be learned in either order. That last question shows that learning graphs branch; they are not just outlines.

**Learning objective (Understand, *explain*):** Explain how learning graphs represent concept dependencies by identifying prerequisite relationships in a visual graph.

## How to Use

1. Click each of the three arrows (the "?" badges) in the graph.
2. Read the panel under the graph to see which concept is the prerequisite.
3. When all three arrows are explored, answer the five questions.
4. Select **Check Answers**. Adjust wrong answers and check again; the summary shows the full learning path.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
8 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Learning Graphs and Concept Dependencies sections of Chapter 8

### Activities

1. **Exploration** (2 min): Click all three arrows and read each relationship aloud.
2. **Questions** (3 min): Answer all five questions and record your score.
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Draw a four-concept learning graph for onboarding a new sales rep on your product.

### Common Misconceptions
- Learning graphs are just outlines.
- All concepts are equally important.
- Dependencies are linear.

### Assessment
- All five questions correct.
- Learner can explain why Story Delivery and Story Analytics can be learned in either order.
