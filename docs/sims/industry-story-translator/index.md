## About This MicroSim

The same story structure can work in any industry, but only if it speaks that industry's language. A hospital leader listens for patient outcomes and HIPAA; a bank's risk officer listens for risk management and regulators; a plant manager listens for OEE and safety.

In this MicroSim you translate one story template for three industries. For each one you choose the language, the challenge, and the regulatory reference. Your choices rewrite the story so you can hear whether it sounds like an insider or an outsider. Several distractors are true but generic ("operational efficiency," "business terminology"), because generic language is the most common translation mistake.

**Learning objective (Apply, *adapt*):** Adapt a base story for different industries by selecting industry-specific language, challenges, and regulatory references.

## How to Use

1. Read the story template and the industry situation.
2. Choose a **Language**, **Challenge**, and **Regulatory Reference**.
3. Select **Submit** to see your translated story and feedback. You can resubmit; only your first try counts.
4. Select **Next Industry**. After all three, the summary shows each industry's best choices.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Industry-Specific Stories section of Chapter 11

### Activities

1. **Translating** (5 min): Complete all three industries and read each translated story aloud.
2. **Contrast** (2 min): Submit the manufacturing story with "Operational efficiency." What makes "Production efficiency" stronger?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Translate the template for an industry you sell into that is not in the MicroSim.

### Common Misconceptions
- The same story works across industries.
- Industry terminology is superficial.
- Regulatory references are optional.

### Assessment
- Correct language, challenge, and regulation for all three industries.
- Learner can explain why SOX and GDPR are wrong for the healthcare story.
