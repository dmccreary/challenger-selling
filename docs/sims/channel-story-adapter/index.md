## About This MicroSim

The same customer story has to work in very different places: a follow-up email read on a phone, a live presentation, a two-minute video, and a LinkedIn post competing with an endless feed. Each channel has its own constraints on **length**, **format**, and **focus**.

In this MicroSim you adapt one base story ("60% less downtime, $2M saved") for four channels. After each submission you see a description of the version you designed, plus feedback on any choice that does not fit the channel. The cases are chosen to break two common assumptions: that shorter is always better, and that visuals are optional.

**Learning objective (Apply, *adapt*):** Adapt a story for different channels (email, presentation, video, social media) by selecting appropriate length, format, and focus.

## How to Use

1. Read the base story and the channel situation.
2. Choose a **Length**, **Format**, and **Focus** from the dropdowns.
3. Select **Submit** to see your adapted version and feedback. You can resubmit; only the first try counts.
4. Select **Next Channel**. After all four, the summary shows the best adaptation for each channel.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
10 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Multi-Channel Story Delivery, Email Storytelling, and Presentation Storytelling sections of Chapter 10

### Activities

1. **Adapting** (5 min): Complete all four channels and record your first-try score.
2. **Reflection** (2 min): Video and presentation share the same length and focus. What makes their formats different?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Write the 100-200 word email version of the base story.

### Common Misconceptions
- The same story works across all channels.
- Shorter is always better.
- Visuals are optional.

### Assessment
- Correct length, format, and focus for all four channels.
- Learner can explain why "hook only" is right for social media but wrong for a presentation.
