## About This MicroSim

**Adaptive learning** uses performance data to choose each learner's next step, so struggling students get support and strong students get challenge instead of everyone following one path. The same idea drives adaptive sales training: a rep who fumbles the price objection story should get a different next lesson than one who nails it.

This MicroSim shows three students who took the same Storytelling Fundamentals quiz. Each scenario combines several signals: score, time on task, MicroSim engagement, and stated interest. You choose the remedial, standard, or advanced path. Feedback explains which signals mattered.

**Learning objective (Apply, *design*):** Design adaptive learning paths by selecting content recommendations based on student performance scenarios.

## How to Use

1. Read the student scenario and note every performance signal.
2. Choose one of the three **Adaptive Path** buttons.
3. Select **Submit** for feedback. You can resubmit to compare explanations; only the first try counts.
4. Select **Next Student**. After all three, the summary lists the rule behind each path.

## Lesson Plan

### Audience
Sales professionals, account executives, sales engineers and business development managers.

### Duration
8 minutes

### Prerequisites
- Adaptive Learning section of Chapter 8

### Activities

1. **Routing** (3 min): Route all three students and record your first-try score.
2. **Reflection** (2 min): Scenario 2 scored 85% but is not advanced. Which missing signal would change your decision?
3. **Transfer** (3 min): Write the signals you would use to route sales reps after a storytelling workshop.

### Common Misconceptions
- All students should follow the same path.
- Adaptive learning is only for struggling students.
- Adaptive paths are linear.

### Assessment
- Correct path for all three students.
- Learner can name at least two signals beyond quiz score.
