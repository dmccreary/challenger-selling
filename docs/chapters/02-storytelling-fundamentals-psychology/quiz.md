# Quiz: Storytelling Fundamentals & Psychology

Test your understanding of story structure, memory psychology, and cognitive biases in sales storytelling with these 10 review questions.

---

#### 1. In a sales story framed around the Hero's Journey, who is most often cast as the hero?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The salesperson
2. The mentor or vendor
3. The competitor
4. The customer
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. The customer is the hero who leaves the ordinary world, faces challenges, and returns transformed by the solution. The salesperson often plays the mentor role, while competitors may appear as part of the tension but are not the protagonist.

    **Concept Tested:** Hero's Journey


---

#### 2. What does the Story Memory Effect describe?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Facts are recalled more accurately than stories
2. Information in narrative form is better remembered than non-narrative information
3. Repetition is the only reliable way to improve recall
4. Emotional content always distorts accurate memory
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The Story Memory Effect states that people recall stories more accurately than equivalent lists of facts because narratives provide structure, emotional engagement, and multiple retrieval cues. Facts alone lack these memory-supporting properties.

    **Concept Tested:** Story Memory Effect


---

#### 3. How do well-structured stories help overcome the limits of working memory?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. They organize information into a coherent narrative structure
2. They add vivid details that expand mental capacity
3. They repeat every fact at least three times
4. They replace facts entirely with emotional language
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Working memory can hold only a few pieces of information at once. Stories reduce cognitive load by connecting facts within a familiar narrative structure, making the information easier to process and remember than disconnected bullet points.

    **Concept Tested:** Working Memory


---

#### 4. Which type of memory stores personal experiences and specific events rather than general facts?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Semantic Memory
2. Procedural Memory
3. Episodic Memory
4. Short-Term Memory
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Episodic memory stores personal experiences and events in context, which is why stories that simulate experiences are so memorable. Semantic memory stores general knowledge and facts, while short-term memory is a temporary holding system.

    **Concept Tested:** Episodic Memory


---

#### 5. Loss aversion means that people tend to:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Prefer the current state of affairs
2. Accept the first piece of information they receive
3. Seek information that confirms their beliefs
4. Feel losses more strongly than equivalent gains
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Loss aversion is the tendency to prefer avoiding losses over acquiring equivalent gains. The pain of losing something is psychologically about twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining something of equal value, making loss-framed stories highly persuasive.

    **Concept Tested:** Loss Aversion


---

#### 6. A CFO has reviewed 12 proposals and asks you to simplify your message to three key points. Which bias is most likely influencing her?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Anchoring Effect
2. Confirmation Bias
3. Decision Fatigue
4. Status Quo Bias
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Decision fatigue occurs when the quality of decisions deteriorates after many choices. The CFO is mentally depleted from reviewing numerous proposals, so a concise narrative with three key points will be easier to process than dense detail.

    **Concept Tested:** Decision Fatigue


---

#### 7. Status quo bias is best defined as:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Judging likelihood based on vivid examples
2. Preferring the current state of affairs
3. Relying on the first piece of information
4. Seeking information that supports existing beliefs
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Status quo bias is the preference for the current state and resistance to change, even when alternatives are objectively better. Stories showing peers successfully making similar changes can reduce this bias by making change feel normal and achievable.

    **Concept Tested:** Status Quo Bias


---

#### 8. The anchoring effect occurs when people:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Rely too heavily on the first piece of information they receive
2. Prefer avoiding losses over making gains
3. Resist change because the current state feels safe
4. Judge events by how easily examples come to mind
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The anchoring effect happens when initial information becomes a reference point for later judgments. Sales professionals can use stories to set favorable anchors, such as industry norms or competitor costs, before presenting their own proposals.

    **Concept Tested:** Anchoring Effect


---

#### 9. A sales story begins with an impressive customer outcome and then explains how that outcome was achieved. Which narrative structure is being used?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Flashback
2. Chronological order
3. Parallel narrative
4. Linear progression
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Starting with the outcome and then revealing how it was achieved is a flashback structure. This can create immediate interest before walking the audience through the process, whereas chronological order would begin with the initial situation and move forward.

    **Concept Tested:** Narrative Structure


---

#### 10. A sales story emphasizes a competitor's dramatic failure to make a risk feel urgent and likely. Which cognitive bias is the story leveraging?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Confirmation Bias
2. Availability Heuristic
3. Anchoring Effect
4. Choice Architecture
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The availability heuristic leads people to judge likelihood by how easily vivid examples come to mind. A dramatic failure story makes that risk more mentally available, causing the customer to see it as more pressing than abstract statistics would.

    **Concept Tested:** Availability Heuristic
