# Quiz: Objection Handling & Story Portfolio

Test your understanding of sales story components, story-based objection handling, and portfolio management with these 10 review questions.

---

#### 1. What is the correct sequence of the six core sales story components?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Opening Hook → Problem Statement → Agitation → Solution Presentation → Social Proof → Call to Action
2. Problem Statement → Opening Hook → Agitation → Solution Presentation → Social Proof → Call to Action
3. Opening Hook → Solution Presentation → Problem Statement → Agitation → Social Proof → Call to Action
4. Opening Hook → Problem Statement → Solution Presentation → Agitation → Social Proof → Call to Action
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The sequence builds narrative tension and resolution: the hook grabs attention, the problem establishes relevance, agitation deepens urgency, the solution presents the path forward, social proof validates it, and the call to action guides next steps. Reversing agitation and solution, or leading with the problem before the hook, weakens the narrative arc.

    **Concept Tested:** Sales Story Components

#### 2. What is the primary purpose of the Agitation component in a sales story?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. To introduce the product as the solution
2. To show the consequences and impact of the problem
3. To provide testimonials and case study evidence
4. To request a specific next step from the customer
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Agitation answers "so what?" by making the cost of inaction visible. It deepens urgency without being manipulative. The solution component introduces the product, social proof provides evidence, and the call to action requests next steps.

    **Concept Tested:** Agitation


---

#### 3. Why is specific social proof generally more persuasive than generic claims?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It is always shorter than a case study
2. It provides concrete evidence that others achieved similar outcomes
3. It avoids mentioning competitors entirely
4. It relies on emotional language rather than data
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Saying "we helped 47 companies in your industry reduce development cycles by 35%" is more credible than "many customers love our solution" because it gives verifiable specifics. Effective social proof does not avoid competitors by name when relevant, nor does it replace data with emotion.

    **Concept Tested:** Social Proof


---

#### 4. A customer says, "Your solution looks good, but the price is beyond our budget." Which type of objection story is most appropriate?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Timing Objection Story
2. Competitor Objection Story
3. Price Objection Story
4. Risk Objection Story
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A Price Objection Story reframes cost by showing how the total cost of alternatives or the status quo exceeded the upfront investment. It addresses the specific concern about budget by demonstrating the financial return and hidden costs the customer may have missed.

    **Concept Tested:** Price Objection Stories


---

#### 5. A prospect says, "This isn't the right time; we need to wait until next quarter." Which story type directly addresses this concern?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Price Objection Story
2. Authority Objection Story
3. Risk Objection Story
4. Timing Objection Story
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. A Timing Objection Story shows the consequences of delay or the benefits of acting quickly. It counters status quo bias by making the cost of waiting visible, rather than arguing about price, authority, or implementation risk.

    **Concept Tested:** Timing Objection Stories


---

#### 6. A customer is concerned that implementation will disrupt operations. Which objection story should you tell?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Price Objection Story
2. Timing Objection Story
3. Risk Objection Story
4. Authority Objection Story
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. A Risk Objection Story shows how another customer addressed similar implementation concerns and succeeded. It validates the worry rather than dismissing it, then provides evidence that the risk is manageable with proper planning.

    **Concept Tested:** Risk Objection Stories

#### 7. When responding to a competitor objection with a story, which approach is most effective?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Point out the competitor's known weaknesses aggressively
2. Focus on why your solution is better using objective evidence
3. Avoid mentioning the competitor to stay above the fray
4. Share confidential details from the competitor's customers
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. The most effective competitor stories are fair and factual, highlighting your differentiation with objective evidence rather than disparaging rivals or sharing confidential information. Ignoring the competitor fails to address the customer's comparison directly, while aggressive attacks can appear unprofessional and undermine trust.

    **Concept Tested:** Competitor Objection Stories

#### 8. A sales rep responds to a price objection with a story about a customer who implemented quickly. The customer remains unconvinced. What is the most likely problem?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The story did not match the specific objection raised
2. The opening hook was too surprising
3. The social proof was too specific
4. The call to action was too aggressive
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. The rep used a story about implementation speed rather than cost or ROI, so it failed to address the price concern. Objection stories must match the objection type. A surprising hook, specific proof, or direct call to action would not explain the mismatch.

    **Concept Tested:** Story Objection Handling

#### 9. Why should a story library use consistent tags and metadata across all stories?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. To enable quick retrieval and team-wide consistency
2. To make every story the same length
3. To eliminate the need for a call to action
4. To prevent any story from being updated
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Consistent tags and metadata let sales professionals find the right story quickly by persona, industry, or objection type. They do not standardize length, remove calls to action, or prevent updates; in fact, versioning and analytics depend on good metadata.

    **Concept Tested:** Story Metadata


---

#### 10. A story consistently captures attention and receives positive feedback, but it rarely leads to next steps. Which component most likely needs refinement?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Opening Hook
2. Agitation
3. Social Proof
4. Call to Action
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. If the story engages and validates but does not advance the sale, the call to action is probably vague, missing, or mismatched to the sales stage. The opening hook, agitation, and social proof appear to be working because the story holds attention and feels credible.

    **Concept Tested:** Call to Action
