# Quiz: Story Effectiveness & Analytics

Test your understanding of story impact metrics, A/B testing, sales cycle analysis, and storytelling ROI with these 10 review questions.

---

#### 1. Which category of metrics measures how well customers remember stories over time?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Engagement Metrics
2. Retention Metrics
3. Conversion Metrics
4. Pipeline Velocity Metrics
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Retention Metrics track story recall after 24 hours, one week, and whether customers retell the story to colleagues. Engagement metrics measure attention during delivery, conversion metrics measure action, and pipeline velocity measures speed through the sales cycle.

    **Concept Tested:** Retention Metrics


---

#### 2. Conversion metrics in storytelling primarily measure:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Whether customers remember the story details
2. How long customers spend listening to the story
3. Whether stories lead to desired next steps or signed agreements
4. How quickly stories move deals through the pipeline
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Conversion metrics connect stories to action, such as scheduling follow-ups, accepting proposals, or closing deals. Memory corresponds to retention, attention to engagement, and speed to pipeline velocity.

    **Concept Tested:** Conversion Metrics


---

#### 3. When running an A/B test on a sales story, why should you change only one variable at a time?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. It guarantees a statistically significant result
2. It reduces the number of prospects needed
3. It prevents customers from noticing the difference
4. It isolates which change caused any difference in outcome
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. Changing one variable at a time lets you attribute performance differences to that specific change. Changing multiple variables simultaneously makes it impossible to know what drove the result, regardless of sample size or customer awareness.

    **Concept Tested:** A/B Testing Stories


---

#### 4. A sales leader wants to know if stories are shortening the sales cycle. Which metric should she track?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Engagement Metrics
2. Retention Metrics
3. Pipeline Velocity
4. Deal Size Impact
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Pipeline Velocity measures how quickly opportunities move through the pipeline, including average cycle length and stage stall rates. Engagement and retention do not measure speed, and deal size impact measures transaction value, not duration.

    **Concept Tested:** Pipeline Velocity


---

#### 5. A team wants to know whether customers still recall the Challenger insight one week after hearing the story. Which metric applies?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Engagement Metrics
2. Retention Metrics
3. Conversion Metrics
4. Deal Size Impact
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **B**. Retention Metrics assess memory over time, including delayed recall and whether the customer shares the story with others. Engagement would measure immediate reaction, while conversion and deal size measure business outcomes.

    **Concept Tested:** Retention Metrics


---

#### 6. A company compares win rates for deals where a specific story was used versus deals where it was not. Which metric category is being applied?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Conversion Metrics
2. Engagement Metrics
3. Retention Metrics
4. Story Benchmarking
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Win rate is a conversion outcome that shows whether the story influenced the final decision. Engagement and retention measure earlier-stage effects, while benchmarking compares performance against an external or internal standard.

    **Concept Tested:** Conversion Metrics


---

#### 7. Pipeline velocity is best defined as:

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. The speed at which opportunities move through the sales pipeline
2. The total revenue in the pipeline
3. The number of stories told per opportunity
4. The average length of a sales story
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Pipeline velocity measures how quickly deals progress through stages. Total revenue describes pipeline value, stories per opportunity measures usage, and story length is a content characteristic, not a pipeline metric.

    **Concept Tested:** Pipeline Velocity


---

#### 8. A story consistently holds attention and generates follow-up questions, but it rarely leads to next steps. Which element is the best candidate for A/B testing?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Opening Hook
2. Agitation
3. Social Proof
4. Call to Action
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **D**. If engagement is high but conversion is low, the call to action is likely unclear, weak, or misaligned with the sales stage. The hook, agitation, and social proof appear to be working because the story captures attention and feels credible.

    **Concept Tested:** Story Optimization


---

#### 9. Why is story attribution difficult in complex B2B sales cycles?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Stories are always too short to measure
2. CRM systems cannot log stories
3. Multiple stories are told across many meetings with many stakeholders
4. Customers never reveal which story influenced them
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **C**. Complex sales involve many interactions and decision-makers, making it hard to isolate which story influenced the outcome. Stories can be logged in CRMs, customers can sometimes identify influential stories, and length is not the core attribution challenge.

    **Concept Tested:** Story Attribution


---

#### 10. To justify continued investment in story development and management, which analysis is most directly useful?

<div class="upper-alpha" markdown>
1. Story ROI Calculation
2. Story Benchmarking
3. Story Competitive Analysis
4. Engagement Metrics
</div>

??? question "Show Answer"
    The correct answer is **A**. Story ROI calculation compares the investment in story development to the returned revenue or efficiency gains, directly justifying resources. Benchmarking, competitive analysis, and engagement metrics provide context but do not quantify return on investment.

    **Concept Tested:** Story ROI Calculation
